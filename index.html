The format

PechaKucha

Born in Tokyo in 2003. Twenty images, twenty seconds each. The timer never stops.

The clock

Why twenty seconds works

Slides move whether you are ready or not. No rambling, no dead air, no going back.

The idea

Start with one sentence

"A site where my students can book a class." That is the whole brief. Everything grows from it.

The assistant

Talk to AI like a helpful human

Super helpful, really keen, and it has no idea what you want until you say it. Context is everything.

The plan

Nail down every detail

Colors, pages, tone, what happens when someone clicks. Ten minutes of answers saves hours of rework.

The build

Hand it to the agent

One prompt starts the build. Then you watch it work, like a pair programmer who never blinks.

The build

Watch it come together

Files appear, styles land, the preview updates. Minutes, not weeks.

The toolchain

GitHub holds everything

Every version, every change, one repo. One git push is the whole delivery system.

The toolchain

Vercel deploys on push

Connect the repo once. Every push builds a live site with its own URL.

The moment

Push, and it is live

The deploy bar finishes, and the site comes into focus. No servers to rent, nothing to install.

The design

Keep it simple

One page can say enough. Plenty of air, one clear action, big type.

The pictures

Photos do the heavy lifting

Free, high-quality images from Unsplash. Drop the link in the prompt and the agent does the rest.

The rhythm

Small steps, deployed often

Add a feature, deploy, look at it live. Fix, deploy again. Ten small wins beat one big gamble.

The reality

Mistakes are normal

Something breaks, you say so, the agent fixes it. Every error comes with a next step.

The payoff

Your first site, tonight

An idea, a laptop, and forty minutes. You walk out with something live and shareable.
